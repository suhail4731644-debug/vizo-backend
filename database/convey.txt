 S1.  THE CHAIN IS SEVEN STEPS, AND DISPATCH FINALLY MOVES THE STOCK
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

     Draft · Submitted · Confirmed · Invoiced/Edit ·
     Processing in Order Dept · Dispatched · Delivered

 "Seen by Warehouse", "On way to Order Dept" and "Packaging" are gone from the
 workflow, from every screen and from the database. So are the two 2026
 leftovers PROCESSING and PACKED, which were already dead.

 SEVEN LIVE ORDERS WERE STANDING IN THE STEPS THAT WENT -- six at "On way to
 Order Dept" and one at "Packaging". They are at "Processing in Order Dept"
 now, and each one has a line in its own history saying the step it was in no
 longer exists, so nobody finds an order that changed state overnight and
 cannot explain it.

 AND THE BIG ONE. Until today nothing in the chain ever took stock off a
 shelf: an order could be invoiced, dispatched and delivered and the shelf
 count would not have moved by a single piece. That was P0 in this file on
 20 September, and it was the largest correctness problem in the system.

 Pressing Dispatched now asks WHICH PLACE the goods are going out of --
 warehouses, order departments and shops, never Claim Stock -- and takes them
 off that shelf:

   · it refuses if that place is short, and says which items and by how many
   · the order's own location is set to the place it really went out of
   · a SALE movement is written per line, so the stock history explains itself
   · the order desk, the accountant and you may do it; nobody else

 WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO: the 26 past orders that were dispatched or delivered
 without ever moving stock are still wrong. Fixing them is a one-off
 correction script, it is ready to write, and I did not run it because it
 changes the shelf count of every product on those orders. Say the word.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 S2.  THE PACKING SCREEN IS RETIRED, AND DISPATCH MOVED BEHIND THE CHAIN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 /packing was the pre-chain way of working: its own queue, its own PACKED
 status, and -- until today -- THE ONLY PLACE IN THE WHOLE APPLICATION WHERE
 STOCK EVER LEFT for a sale. It sat beside the ten-step chain built on
 3 September and the two never met.

 With the stock coming off at Dispatched, two screens taking the same goods
 off the same shelf is how a count goes wrong, so packing stops: the page, the
 menu entry and the route are gone, and its API answers with a sentence saying
 where the work moved to rather than a 404.

 /dispatch now sits AFTER the dispatch step instead of before it. It is where
 the courier, the bilty, the parcels and the COD are recorded once the goods
 are on a van; it no longer changes the order's status, because the chain
 already did.

 THE WAREHOUSE SCREEN IS NOW A LIST, NOT A QUEUE. The keeper's two buttons
 were the two steps that went. /warehouse still shows every invoiced order
 with what is on it, what is on the shelf and the bill to check against --
 which is what the keeper actually needs -- and the order desk moves the order
 on when it takes it up.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 S3.  TAKING AN ORDER: ONE SCREEN, PICTURES, AND A MARGIN
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 The three-step wizard is one page. It stopped asking three things:

   "Selling from"   asked on the day the order was taken, which is before
                    anybody knows. It is asked at dispatch now (S1).
   "Sales rep"      a dropdown of everybody. The order is recorded against
                    whoever is signed in, always -- and the API ignores
                    anything a browser sends, so it cannot be worked around.
                    You and the accountant still see whose order it is on the
                    order itself and in every list.
   Disc % / Tax %   per line. The rate IS the price agreed; the tax is the
                    product's own rate and is applied without being asked for.

 IN THEIR PLACE, per line: Qty · RATE · Margin · Margin %.

 Margin and margin % are linked, and both move the rate, because the rate is
 landed cost plus margin:

     Titan AirPro: cost 720 (cost + duty), rate 1,280 -> margin 560
     type 50 % -> margin 360, rate 1,080
     type a margin of 200 -> 27.78 %, rate 920
     type a rate of 900 -> margin 180, 25 %

 It is the same arithmetic as the product screen, out of the same file, so the
 two can never tell different stories about one item. A rate below what the
 piece cost to land turns the line red and is still allowed -- clearance is
 real.

 AND THE PICTURES. The item picker leads with the photograph and every line on
 the order carries its own, because that is how an order is actually taken:
 the rep shows the shopkeeper a picture and the shopkeeper points at it.

 The customer picker shows the DISPLAY name in full, not cut short, because
 the display name is what tells four Ahmeds apart.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 S4.  A CUSTOMER IS CALLED BY THEIR DISPLAY NAME NOW, EVERYWHERE
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 128 places in the API were handing out the registered name. Orders, invoices,
 returns, deliveries, claims, statements, reports, the dashboards, the
 notifications -- all of them now read "display name, or the legal name when
 there is none".

 TWO PLACES KEEP THE LEGAL NAME, on purpose:

   · the customer's own record, where both are shown -- it is the field you
     typed, and the screen that edits it must show what it is editing
   · printed documents, where the counterparty block carries the registered
     name with the display name above it. A credit note that names a party
     differently from the invoice it credits is an argument waiting to happen.

 The company's own legal name on the letterhead is untouched -- that is the
 "Company" table, a different thing entirely.

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 S6.  OPENING A CUSTOMER FROM THEIR PAPERWORK
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 /parties/new?type=customer is two pages now.

 PAGE ONE -- the documents, one set at a time, in this order:

     CNIC            front and back
     Business card   front and back
     Affidavit       page 1 and page 2

 Each picture can be taken with the camera (it opens inside the page, back
 camera on a phone, webcam on a laptop) or uploaded -- and Upload on a phone
 is the one that offers the gallery, which is where the picture usually
 already is. Every set has "Not available", and skipping all three goes
 straight to the form with empty boxes, which is exactly what the old screen
 was.

 THE PICTURES GO UP AS THEY ARE TAKEN, to the images Cloudinary account. They
 are not held in the browser: six photographs in a form is a save that fails
 on a bad line with everything lost.

 PAGE TWO -- the form, with whatever could be read already in the boxes:

     Legal Name    the CNIC's own name, WITHOUT the father's name
     Display Name  cnic name - shop name - shop location, the shop name and
                   the location from the BUSINESS CARD
     Category      Retailer by default
     Industry      the shop's name with its location
     Phone / Alt   from the CNIC; both when two are printed, in either
                   03xxxxxxxxx or +923xxxxxxxxx form
     Email         from the business card, any domain
     Address       from the BUSINESS CARD, never the CNIC -- goods go to the
                   shop, not to where the man sleeps
     City          from the CNIC, matched to one of your eleven Pakistani
                   cities (they are stored "Karachi - Pakistan", so the match
                   is on what comes before the dash), and the province follows

 ENGLISH ONLY. A CNIC prints every field in Urdu as well; the Urdu is ignored
 rather than transliterated, because a transliteration is a guess dressed up
 as a fact. Placeholder text like "xyz" is skipped.

 NOTHING IS SAVED UNTIL THE SALESPERSON HAS READ IT. Every filled box is
 editable, and what they save is what the database gets. The reader is a
 typist with good eyes, not a source of truth.

 A PICTURE THAT CANNOT BE READ is named. The screen goes back to that section
 and says which: "not clear", "too much light", "part of the document is
 outside the picture". It does not half-fill the form -- a name that is 80 %
 right is worse than an empty box, because nobody re-reads a box that is
 already filled.

 THE ONE PDF. After the customer is created, every picture they gave is bound
 into a single legal_documents.pdf -- a cover page with who they are and what
 is in the set, then one page per photograph -- and stored on the documents
 Cloudinary account with its link on the customer. Anybody who can open the
 customer can download it.

 EDITING NEVER RE-READS. Adding a picture to an existing customer files it and
 rebuilds the PDF; it does not touch the details. Once a person has checked a
 name, a machine does not get another go at it -- that was your instruction
 and it is also the right way round.

 THE CITY LIST ON THE CUSTOMER FORM IS PAKISTAN ONLY now. The Chinese cities
 are still there for suppliers, which is what they were added for.

 CATEGORY BY ROLE: a rep and the accountant may open a Retailer, a Wholesaler
 or an Agent. Distributor and Manufacturer are yours alone. The list is cut on
 the server, so it cannot be widened by editing the page.

 R5.  THE SALES RETURN, REBUILT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 WHO. Only the accountant and you. The screen is gone from the salesman's
 sidebar, typing the address sends him to the 403 page, and every returns
 endpoint refuses his token — permission AND role, both. The order desk lost it
 as well, because you said "only admin and accountant".

 THE FLOW, in the order you described it:

   1  SALESPERSON, optional. Only narrows the customer list to his.
   2  CUSTOMER, required. Everything below comes from this one choice.
   3  On the right: HIS LAST 5 ORDERS with date, order and invoice number, and
      every item with its quantity — and under it EVERYTHING HE HAS EVER
      BOUGHT, from his first bill to his last, with how many were bought, how
      many have already come back and how many still may. No date window at
      all, exactly as you asked.
   4  In the middle: ADD PRODUCT, which offers ONLY what this customer has been
      billed for. Anything already returned in full is greyed out.
   5  WHERE THE GOODS GO — asked, never guessed. One location for the return,
      and every returned piece is added to it the moment you save.
   6  Save. The date is today (system date, not typeable), the credit note is
      generated and stored, and the stock is on the shelf before the screen
      moves.

 TAPPING IS FASTER THAN TYPING. A line in the "last 5 orders" panel adds that
 item with the quantity that was sold on that order; a line in the "everything
 bought" list adds one. Both respect the ceiling.

 THE CEILING, which you were most particular about: bought minus already
 returned, per item, per customer. Five hundred bought means at most five
 hundred back, and 501 is refused. It is checked in four places, on purpose:

     · the + and − buttons stop at it
     · a typed number is clamped to it
     · adding the same item twice sums the two rather than letting each pass
     · and the API recomputes the whole thing INSIDE the transaction that
       writes the return, under a per-customer lock, so two people saving at
       the same second cannot both get through

 Tested against the live database, every path: 33 of 32 refused, an item the
 customer never bought refused, zero and negative quantities refused, no items
 refused, a closed location refused, a salesman's token refused.

 THE PRICE. Each piece is credited at what THAT customer actually paid for it
 on average — discount taken off, tax on — across every bill they have had.
 That was your answer to the question; the other options were the latest price
 they paid, or today's catalogue price. Nobody can type a rate on this screen,
 which is deliberate: a refund amount typed into a browser is a refund amount
 somebody can change.

 DAMAGED GOODS. There is no "condition" question any more. The shelf answers
 it: send them to Claim Stock and the return records them as damaged and says
 so on the screen; send them anywhere else and they are resalable. Either way
 the pieces land where you said they should, which is what you asked for.

 NO APPROVAL STEP. The return is POSTED the moment it is saved, because the

 P1.  THE SKU IS MADE, NOT TYPED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 Your example was "VZ-TIT-T9-EAR". Your own description of the format also
 asked for the colour and a serial, so both are there: BLK, and 01. The serial
 is what keeps it unique: two different products whose names reduce to the
 same parts — "VIZO VOLT Car Charger 45W" and a "VIZO VOLT Wall Charger 45W",
 say — are VZ-VOL-45W-CHA-01 and VZ-VOL-45W-CHA-02.

 THE JUDGEMENT CALLS, all in backend Services/SkuGenerator.cs, one file, so
 they can be argued with:

   · The brand word is skipped (VZ already says it).
   · Descriptions are never the WORD: Wireless, Bluetooth, Smart, Fast, Type…
     "Wireless Earbuds T9" is a T9, and "WIR" tells nobody anything.
   · The category's own words are never the WORD either, or every earbud would
     be VZ-EAR-…
   · MODEL is the first token with letters AND digits (T9, V65, X2, VPD45W).
   · No model number? A rating stands in: 10000mAh -> 10K, 65W -> 65W. The
     10,000 and 20,000 mAh power banks differ in nothing but that.
   · COLOUR is what follows a dash ("… - Black"), or a colour word anywhere.
     Two-word shades keep their first letter: Sky Blue -> SBLU, Rose Gold ->
     RGLD, Cool White -> CWHT.

 A rep raises it against one of their own invoices. The API refuses an invoice
 that is not theirs, refuses a product that is not on it, and refuses more
 units than were sold less whatever has already come back.

 IT GETS A DOCUMENT OF ITS OWN — a return note, not a second invoice. That is
 the distinction you drew and it is the right one: the order keeps the single
 bill it was billed on, because that number is printed on paper the customer is
 holding and a second bill for the same goods is how one sale gets paid for
 twice. What comes back is a different event with its own number (SR-…) and its
 own piece of paper.

 The note carries the invoice AND the order it came off, every line with its
 CONDITION, where each resalable unit went back to, and the credit due. The
 condition is on the face of it because it is the whole argument: a resalable
 unit is worth its price again, a damaged one is a loss somebody absorbs.

 It is archived to Cloudinary the moment the return is saved, in the same store
 and through the same renderer as every other document. Returns raised BEFORE
 today have no archived copy; their Print button renders from the database, so
 it works, and archives nothing.

 YOU ARE TOLD BY NAME. The notification goes to you, to Accounts and to the
 order desk (stock is coming back to a shelf they look after). Every copy now
 ends with who it was addressed to — see section 5.

 ON YOUR DASHBOARD it is a column of its own, in red, and a link. Count of
 returns, how many are waiting on a decision, units back, value credited, and
 the five most recent with the ORDER number rather than just the invoice —
 because the question you asked is which orders had goods come back. Clicking
 through goes to the list; clicking a row goes to the return, where the order,
 the bill, how many of the sold units came back, both PDFs and the whole
 activity trail are on one screen.

 21 SEP, LATER — WHAT TESTING FOUND, AND WHAT WAS DONE ABOUT IT

 Three things were broken and are fixed; the rest is for you to know.

 1. EVERY AI REPORT WENT SILENT THE FIRST TIME GEMINI SAID "BUSY".
    Services/GeminiClient.ExplainAsync -- behind the sales-drop explanation, who
    to chase for money, dead stock, customers at risk, the demand forecast, margin

    (PostAsync) used by both, and a 429 (out of quota) moves straight to the next
    model instead of waiting a second on one that will not answer until tomorrow.
    The nightly insight goes through the same method; it runs at its own hour, so

    PartiesController.Get built a "documents" block (the six picture links and the

 THE FRONT / BACK CHECK, and how far it goes:

    Three layers, cheapest first, because each catches what the one before cannot.
      a) In the browser, before anything is uploaded: a fingerprint of the picture
         (lib/image-hash.ts) compared with the other side. The SAME FILE, and a
         second photograph of the same face, are refused with "the front and back
         pictures are very much the same". Nothing is uploaded for a refused one.
      b) On the API, for byte-identical files: no AI call is spent on it.
      c) On the API, by the AI: it reads WHICH face of the CNIC each picture is
         (photograph and name = front; address and barcode = back), and refuses a
         picture in the wrong slot -- including the sides swapped.
    The AI is told the pictures by neutral names, "cnicPictureOne" and "Two". It
    used to be told "cnicFront" and "cnicBack" and simply believed the labels, so
    swapped sides sailed through. That was found by testing exactly that case.

    THE THRESHOLDS WERE MEASURED, NOT GUESSED, and are different per document
    (lib/image-hash.ts explains the figures). The reason to know: two different
    pages of a typed affidavit are already only 10 bits of 64 apart, so a single
    strict number would have refused a perfectly good page 2. The affidavit is
    only caught if it is the same file (or very nearly); the CNIC is caught at 15.
